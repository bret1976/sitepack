      <p class="dropcap">REMARKABLE scenes attended the composing room of this journal late yesterday evening, when a calculating engine of the newest design was observed to set, justify, and correct the whole of the present edition without the assistance of a single human compositor. The device, described by its keepers as a "language model" — a term this newspaper does not pretend to understand — worked through the night without complaint, tobacco, or wages, three habits hitherto thought inseparable from the printing trade.</p>
      <p>Foreman R. Whitlock, twenty-two years at the stone, examined the machine's justification and pronounced it "suspiciously even." The apprentices, less charitable, have accused the engine of showing off. It is reported that when asked for a comment, the machine produced a sonnet, which the editors have declined to print on grounds of space, envy, or both.</p>
      <p>The proprietors wish to assure subscribers that the engine has been instructed in the customs of this house: no scandal above the fold, no adjectives before breakfast, and absolutely no poetry in the shipping news.</p>
